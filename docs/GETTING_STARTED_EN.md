# Getting Started with Mi grado

Mi grado is an experimental study notebook for university and vocational students. It helps you organize subjects and tasks, record your study attempts, and continue where you left off. You can use the basic features without Obsidian or AI.

**Important:** The interface is currently in Spanish. The app is experimental, so use fictional data when testing and keep a backup of your notebook.

## 1. Open the web app

Open the [Mi grado web app](https://aybar17.github.io/mi-grado-app/app/) in your browser.

## 2. Create a subject

1. Open **Plan de estudio** (Study Plan).
2. Select **Añadir mi asignatura** (Add my subject).
3. Enter a subject name. Notes are optional.

For example, create a fictional subject called “Introduction to Python.”

## 3. Add a task and record your progress

1. Open **Mis tareas** (My Tasks) and add a task.
2. Enter the time you have available to study today.
3. Prepare an exercise, or open **Registrar avance** (Record Progress).
4. Record what you tried, what help you received, the result, and your next step.

You can organize your work without providing an exercise topic. To ask the AI to solve an exercise, provide the actual exercise statement.

## 4. Save and recover your notebook

1. Open **Copias** (Backups) and download a JSON backup.
2. Check that the downloaded file is in your Downloads folder.
3. You can also export Markdown to read your notes or use them elsewhere.
4. Keep your backup safe. If you need to restore a JSON backup, use the app's restore feature and check the restored notebook.

The web app stores your notebook in your browser on that device. It does not automatically synchronize data between devices. Browser storage can be deleted, so keep backups.

## 5. Important limitations

- The interface is currently in Spanish.
- The app is experimental, and independent recovery checks on other devices are still pending.
- Obsidian and AI are optional; the basic study notebook works without them.
- Recording a task or explanation does not prove that you have mastered a subject.

Use fictional data while testing. Never share private notes, passwords, or API keys.
